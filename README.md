# E-Commerce Platform | Business Analysis Case Study

An end-to-end Business Analysis portfolio case study for a fictional mid-sized retail business moving from manual sales operations to an online e-commerce platform.

This project demonstrates how a Business Analyst can translate a business problem into documented requirements, process models, user stories, wireframes, and Agile delivery artifacts.

> **Portfolio-project note:** This is a simulated case study, not a live client engagement or deployed e-commerce product. Business scenarios, requirements, and performance figures are illustrative. Any percentage-based outcomes shown in the interactive dashboard are assumptions or proposed targets, not measured results from a real implementation.

---

## 1. Business Problem

The fictional retail business relies on manual sales and order-handling processes. The proposed online channel is intended to address these challenges:

- Manual order processing and avoidable errors
- Delays in order fulfilment
- Limited payment options
- Poor visibility into inventory availability
- Lack of customer-facing order tracking
- Customer experience issues across the purchase journey

The case study defines the business and system requirements for a proposed e-commerce platform.

## 2. Business Objectives

- Enable customers to discover and search for products online.
- Improve the shopping and checkout experience.
- Support multiple payment methods.
- Reduce manual effort in order processing.
- Provide order-status visibility and tracking.
- Establish measurable customer-experience and operational goals.

## 3. Scope and Modules

The proposed platform covers:

- Product catalogue, search, and filters
- User registration and login
- Cart management
- Checkout and payment gateway
- Order management and tracking
- Wishlist
- Reviews and ratings
- Customer support
- Analytics dashboard

## 4. Business Analysis Deliverables

The portfolio contains the following artifacts:

- Business Requirements Document (BRD) summary
- Functional Requirements Document (FRD) summary
- Functional and non-functional requirements
- User stories with acceptance criteria
- Business objectives and prioritisation
- Process-flow diagrams
- Low-fidelity UI wireframes
- Sprint planning board
- Requirement Traceability Matrix (RTM)
- Stakeholder analysis
- Gap-analysis considerations

## 5. Agile and Requirements Approach

The case study uses an illustrative Agile/Scrum delivery approach:

- Three planned sprints
- User stories and story points
- Product-backlog structure
- Acceptance criteria
- MoSCoW prioritisation
- Requirement traceability from business objectives to requirements and test considerations

Sprint plans are proposed portfolio artifacts; they do not represent work delivered by an actual development team.

## 9. Key Learning Outcomes

This case study demonstrates the process of moving from a high-level business problem to structured analysis artifacts. It showcases how business objectives can be translated into requirements, user stories, process models, and proposed solution designs.

It is intended to support discussion of Business Analyst and Business Operations skills in a portfolio or interview. It does not claim that the proposed platform was built, deployed, or produced verified business improvements.
